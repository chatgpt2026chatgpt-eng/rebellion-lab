# Project Vision — From an Idea to a Playable Simulation

## How It Started

For years, I had imagined creating a strategy game that combined political leadership, state management, and military conflict. I had a clear vision of the experience I wanted to build, but I lacked the programming skills needed to implement a project of this scale.

The turning point came when I watched YouTube videos demonstrating how artificial intelligence could assist with programming. For the first time, I began to see a practical path toward developing the game I had wanted to create for years.

I started with the free version of ChatGPT, discussing the concept and exploring how its systems might work. After several days, I subscribed to ChatGPT Plus and began turning those discussions into actual development.

Over time, I moved toward more advanced AI-assisted workflows, including ChatGPT Work.

## Learning to Build With AI

My early development process was slow and difficult.

I copied code changes manually, searched through project files, and edited them one at a time. As the game grew, coordinating changes across many interconnected systems became increasingly challenging.

One of the most important improvements came when ChatGPT suggested using executable update scripts, which I call APPLY files.

Instead of manually modifying numerous files, I could run a script that applied coordinated changes to the project.

This transformed my workflow and gave me the confidence to continue building increasingly complex systems.

ChatGPT has assisted with code generation, debugging, visual improvements, system design, and integrating different gameplay mechanics.

However, AI is currently a development tool rather than a model integrated into the game itself. Automated unit behavior and gameplay decisions are implemented through conventional programming.

## The Game I Want to Create

My long-term vision is a strategy game in which political power and military conflict are deeply interconnected.

I want players to experience the responsibilities, pressures, and consequences of governing a country.

Political decisions should have lasting effects. Economic policies may influence public stability. Security measures may protect institutions while also creating risks of abuse. Political compromises may solve one crisis while producing another.

I want the game to explore how power can gradually lead a leader toward corruption, but also how responsible leadership can improve citizens' lives.

The objective is not to tell players that one political choice is always correct. It is to let them experience the consequences of their decisions.

## The Human Cost of War

The military side of the game is intended to be more than a collection of units moving across a battlefield.

Aircraft, armored vehicles, artillery, drones, infantry, and defensive systems should have distinct roles, strengths, and limitations.

Terrain, logistics, intelligence, and tactical positioning should matter.

I also want players to understand the human consequences of military decisions.

A war may begin with a decision made by one person, yet its consequences can affect tens or hundreds of thousands of people.

Military victory should not automatically mean political success. A battlefield decision may have economic, diplomatic, or social consequences long after the fighting ends.

## Rebellion Lab: An Open-Source Experiment

Rebellion Lab is an experimental tactical simulation extracted from the larger game project.

Its current scenario focuses on conflict between government forces and rebel groups.

The prototype brings together ground forces, artillery, drones, aircraft, air defense, terrain, and logistics within a shared battlefield.

The purpose of publishing this isolated laboratory is to make its technical implementation available for inspection, experimentation, feedback, and potential contributions.

The complete political game remains a separate, unpublished project.

## Where I Hope to Go Next

My long-term ambition is to create a political and military strategy experience capable of supporting approximately 15–20 hours or more of meaningful gameplay.

I hope to expand the military simulation beyond rebellion scenarios to conflicts between states, while developing deeper connections between political decisions, economic systems, and military outcomes.

I also want to improve the technical foundations of the project: performance, maintainability, simulation behavior, user experience, and testing.

Open-source development offers an opportunity to learn from other developers and make parts of this work useful beyond my own game.

## What AI Has Made Possible

The most meaningful achievement of this journey is not simply the number of systems or lines of code created.

It is that an idea I had considered beyond my technical abilities has become a working, playable prototype.

AI-assisted development helped me cross the gap between imagining a game and beginning to build one.

I am still learning, and the project remains experimental. But the experience has shown me how powerful these tools can be for independent creators who have ambitious ideas and limited programming experience.
